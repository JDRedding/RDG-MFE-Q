## Abstract

Dynamics obey a Fermat Principle: trajectories minimize a coherence-weighted action rather than Euclidean length. This variational perspective allows geometry to emerge from the closure flow Γ, with distances and geodesics determined by dynamical connectivity and coherence. It reverses the classical Riemannian paradigm in which a metric is prescribed *a priori*. The resulting closure-generated geometry aligns naturally with Lagrangian Coherent Structures, transfer-operator methods, and Conley-type invariants, offering a unified dynamical-first framework for coherent organization.

## 2. Intuition and Analogy

Classical geometry begins with a manifold equipped with a metric, then studies dynamics upon it. **Closure-generated geometry** inverts the order: the dynamics Γ induces the geometry. The effective structure arises from invariant sets, basins of attraction, coherence measures µ, and stability properties of Γ.

This perspective resonates with Lagrangian Coherent Structures (LCS), dynamic embeddings, and ergodic transport theory.

### Fermat-Type Variational Principle

The starting point of the RDG framework is a Fermat-type principle for dynamical systems. Instead of minimizing Euclidean arclength, trajectories extremize a **coherence-weighted action**.

Let \( C_\Gamma(x, \dot{x}) \) denote the coherence density induced by the closure flow Γ. The action functional is defined by
\[
S[x] = \int_0^T C_\Gamma(x(t), \dot{x}(t)) \, dt.
\]
A trajectory \( x(t) \) obeys the **RDG Fermat Principle** if it satisfies \( \delta S[x] = 0 \).

### Coherence-Weighted Action

Let \( C_\Gamma : TP \to [0,\infty) \) be the coherence density. For an absolutely continuous curve \( x : [0,T] \to P \),
\[
S[x] = \int_0^T C_\Gamma(x(t), \dot{x}(t)) \, dt.
\]
\( C_\Gamma(x, \dot{x}) \) acts as a **refractive index**: high coherence regions cause trajectories to bend toward dynamically coherent structures.

## 3. Closure Flow Γ

A closure flow is a one-parameter semigroup \( \Gamma = \{\Gamma_t\}_{t\geq 0} \) with \( \Gamma_0 = \mathrm{id} \) and \( \Gamma_{t+s} = \Gamma_t \circ \Gamma_s \), acting on the primitive state space \( P \).

The flow Γ serves two roles:
1. Determines dynamical connectivity.
2. Generates the coherence density \( C_\Gamma(x, \dot{x}) \) that shapes the emergent geometry.

## 4. Emergent Geometry

**RDG inverts the classical paradigm**:
> Dynamics ⟹ Coherence ⟹ Action ⟹ Geometry

The geometry is **closure-generated**: distances, neighborhoods, and distinguished directions arise from the variational structure of the action.

### RDG Distance

\[
d_\Gamma(A,B) := \inf_{\substack{x(0)=A \\ x(T)=B}} \int_0^T C_\Gamma(x(t), \dot{x}(t)) \, dt
\]

A curve attaining this infimum is a **closure geodesic**.

### Closure Geodesics

A curve \( \gamma : [0,T] \to P \) is a closure geodesic if it locally minimizes \( S[\gamma] \).

### Euler–Lagrange Equations

Assuming \( C_\Gamma \) is continuously differentiable,
\[
\frac{d}{dt} \left( \frac{\partial C_\Gamma}{\partial \dot{x}} \right) - \frac{\partial C_\Gamma}{\partial x} = 0.
\]

1. Coherent regions ↔ geodesically coherent regions (small RDG distance).

### Double Gyre Flow

In the finite-time periodically forced double gyre, closure geodesics trace coherent gyre cores and avoid the oscillating separatrix — aligning naturally with finite-time LCS.

### Two-Attractor System

The quotient geometry \( P / \sim_\Gamma \) collapses each basin of attraction to a point, with RDG distance measuring the action cost of crossing the basin boundary.
